## Prompt

```
Design "Skyward — Par Avion", a brief history of flight from 1783 to 1969 issued as thirteen commemorative airmail stamps (an invented stamp issue; the flights, dates and people are real). The whole viewport is framed by a red/blue diagonal airmail chevron border (#c8233a / #1f3f99). The ground is pale blue aerogramme paper (#dde7f3) with faint procedural grain; ink navy #1b2a55 for type. The header has a small tilted "PAR AVION / BY AIR MAIL" label, a huge condensed bold title "Skyward" (stack: Bahnschrift, "DIN Condensed", "Roboto Condensed", "Arial Narrow", sans-serif) underlined by a chevron stripe, an italic book-serif subtitle ("Iowan Old Style", Palatino, Georgia, serif), a round instrument-style year dial with a red needle (1780–1970), and a stamp album (13 small squares, "0 of 13 franked", sound toggle, "Clear album").

The main area is a horizontally scrolling sky: a sinuous flight route with the stamps alternating above and below it, each joined to a dot on the route by a dashed leader. The route already flown is drawn as alternating red/blue dashes; the route ahead is a faint dotted line. Two parallax layers of outlined, engraved-style clouds drift at different speeds. A small craft travels along the route at the centre of the viewport, tilting with the curve, and changes by era: a hot-air balloon before 1900, a biplane until 1939, a jet until 1957, then a rocket. On load, the route draws itself while the balloon glides in from the left.

Each milestone is a postage stamp with real perforated edges (CSS mask), sized to multiples of 12px. It has a printed frame and fine engraved hatching in its own ink colour (carmine, sepia, olive, ultramarine, violet, teal, indigo, orange, slate, purple…), the year, a fictional denomination (5c … $1), a line-drawn SVG illustration of the craft, the craft's name in italic serif and "SKYWARD AIR POST". The milestones: 1783 Montgolfier balloon (Pilâtre de Rozier and d'Arlandes, Paris); 1853 Cayley's crewed glider (Brompton Dale); 1891 Lilienthal's gliders; 1903 Wright Flyer (12 s, 120 ft); 1909 Blériot XI across the Channel; 1919 Alcock and Brown's Vickers Vimy (first non-stop transatlantic flight, carrying the first transatlantic airmail); 1927 Lindbergh's Spirit of St. Louis; 1932 Earhart's Lockheed Vega 5B; 1939 Heinkel He 178; 1947 Yeager's Bell X-1; 1957 Sputnik 1; 1961 Gagarin's Vostok 1; 1969 Apollo 11 lunar module Eagle. Keep every claim accurate and well established.

Clicking a stamp opens it as an airmail postcard with a chevron border. The left side shows the large illustration and year with "Plate n of 13". The right side is the address side: a mini stamp, a title, and dotted-rule lines for When / From / To / Craft / Aboard, plus a short original caption. On first opening, a circular cancellation postmark with wavy lines thunks onto the stamp. It carries the landing place along its ring and the arrival date in the centre, rendered in worn violet-black ink via an SVG turbulence filter; the card jolts slightly. Franked stamps show the postmark in the timeline, fill their route dot and album square, and persist in localStorage. The album shows a completion message at 13 of 13.

Sound (Web Audio, off by default, visible toggle): an engine loop that morphs by era — burner whoosh, propeller chop, jet whine, rocket rumble — louder and higher-pitched as you scroll faster, plus a low thunk when a postmark lands.

Navigation: native horizontal scroll, mouse drag, the vertical wheel mapped to horizontal movement, ← → keys stepping between stamps, and prev/next buttons. A bottom year ruler (1780–1970) has coloured milestone dots and a red marker; click or drag to scrub. The postcard supports Esc, ← →, focus trapping and prev/next.

Stamp and route sizes are computed from the available height so everything fits from 360px phones to desktops. Include a dark theme (deep night-blue ground, stamps and postcards stay paper-coloured) and a reduced-motion fallback (no intro, no thunk animation). Pause animation and audio when the tab is hidden.
```
